What you’re seeing

Every star is real and in its real place: about 18,000 of them, everything you could see from Earth with your eyes plus every catalogued star within 80 light years. As the ship moves, each one is redrawn as bright as it would look from where you are, so nearby stars slide across the sky and constellations come apart.

Warp speeds follow the scale where warp 9 is about 1,500 times the speed of light. The trip times are what that would take; the flight is shortened so you don’t have to wait a day.

Star data: the HYG database by David Nash (CC BY-SA 4.0), built from the Hipparcos, Yale Bright Star and Gliese catalogues. Constellation lines and the Milky Way: d3-celestial by Olaf Frohn.
